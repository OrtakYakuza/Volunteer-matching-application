q# All Requirements (Papers+Interviews)

## MOSCOW Definitions
* **Must:** Without it, the prototype cannot meet the thesis goal or cannot be evaluated as planned.
* **Should:** High value, but there is a workaround (manual step, simplified logic).
* **Could:** Useful polish/enhancement if time remains.
* **Won't:** Out of scope for bachelor timeframe; document as future work.

## Functional Requirements (FR)
* **FR-01 Virtual VRC:** The system shall provide a virtual VRC (Volunteer Reception Center) as a web-based platform that enables volunteers to register. [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] **Must**
* **FR-02 Opportunity publishing:** The system shall display current volunteer opportunities/tasks related to the incident. [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] **Must**
* **FR-03 Registration simplicity:** The system shall support rapid registration by collecting only essential volunteer data needed for matching and operations. [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] **Must**
* **FR-04 Self-assessment entry:** The system shall allow volunteers to enter their own registration data (self-assessment). [Towards a Taxonomy for Classification of Coordination Systems, Betke et al.] **Must**
* **FR-05 Coordinator-assisted entry:** The system shall allow coordinators to add volunteers who cannot register themselves. [Towards a Taxonomy for Classification of Coordination Systems, Betke et al.] **Should**
* **FR-06 Task creation:** The system shall allow coordinators to create tasks with schedule, priority, description, activities, per-activity demand, location, and optional meeting point. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Must**
* **FR-07 Task editing:** The system shall allow coordinators to change task information as the situation changes. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Should**
* **FR-08 Matching baseline:** "The system shall implement an algorithmic method to generate volunteer-task assignment proposals." [Volunteering matching application for a crisis management platform, Petrovic] **Must**
* **FR-08a Matching baseline:** The baseline method shall be bipartite matching (or weighted scoring). [Volunteering matching application for a crisis management platform, Petrovic] **Should or Won't**
* **FR-09 Three automation modes:** The system shall support manual, semi-automatic (suggestions for approval), and automatic proposal modes. [Towards a Taxonomy for Classification of Coordination Systems, Betke et al.] [Volunteering matching application for a crisis management platform, Petrovic] **Should**
* **FR-10 Semi-automatic approval:** In semi-automatic mode, the system shall present coordinator-reviewable assignment proposals before notifying volunteers. [Towards a Taxonomy for Classification of Coordination Systems, Betke et al.] **Should if FR-09**
* **FR-11 Volunteer agency:** Regardless of the automation mode, volunteer agency shall be preserved throughout the assignment process. The system shall require volunteers to apply for open tasks themselves, ensuring that no assignment is confirmed without an initial act of self-selection on the volunteer's part. [Towards a Taxonomy for Classification of Coordination Systems, Betke et al.] **Must**
* **FR-12 Capability constraint:** The system shall restrict assignments to volunteers who match the required capability/skill constraints for a task or activity. [Decision Support for the Optimal Coordination of Spontaneous Volunteers, Rauchecker et al.] **Should**
* **FR-13 Availability constraint:** The system shall restrict assignments to time blocks where volunteers are available. [Decision Support for the Optimal Coordination of Spontaneous Volunteers, Rauchecker et al.] [An optimization model for humanitarian relief volunteer management, Falasca et al.] **Should**
* **FR-14 No double-booking:** The system shall prevent assigning a volunteer to multiple concurrent task activities. [Decision Support for the Optimal Coordination of Spontaneous Volunteers, Rauchecker et al.] **Should**
* **FR-15 Workload cap (burnout):** The system shall allow coordinators to set and enforce a maximum number of shifts/time blocks per volunteer within a planning horizon. [An optimization model for humanitarian relief volunteer management, Falasca et al.] [Decision Support for the Optimal Coordination of Spontaneous Volunteers, Rauchecker et al.] **Could**
* **FR-16 Balance shortages:** The system shall support balancing unmet demand (shortages) across tasks to avoid critical gaps. [An optimization model for humanitarian relief volunteer management, Falasca et al.] **Should**
* **FR-17 Load-balanced allocation:** The system shall support distributing volunteers across active tasks to reduce "overrun" at some sites while other sites remain in urgent need. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Should**
* **FR-18 Coordinator status monitoring:** The system shall allow volunteers to manually button-click their status changes (e.g., 'I have arrived', 'Job Done') in the web UI. **Could | Won't**
* **FR-19 Need-to-know volunteer UI:** The volunteer UI shall display assignment-specific mission details and avoid presenting extra disaster information that could encourage unsafe independent action. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Should**
* **FR-20 Assignment details view:** The system shall allow volunteers to view their current assignment/mission details at any time. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Should**
* **FR-21 Acknowledgement of offer:** After submitting availability/skills, the system shall acknowledge receipt to the volunteer. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Should**
* **FR-22 One-to-many messaging:** The system shall allow coordinators to send task alerts/details to all matched volunteers or selected groups at once. [Towards a Taxonomy for Classification of Coordination Systems, Betke et al.] **Won't**
* **FR-23 Communication back-channel:** The system shall provide a mechanism to communicate back with registered volunteers (e.g., email notifications or web UI, tbd...). [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] [A Graph-Based Approach to Volunteer Management, Windsperger] **Could**
* **FR-24 Documentation workflow:** The system shall support documenting referrals/assignments, volunteer activities, and volunteer hours for reporting and reimbursement needs. [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] **Could**
* **FR-25 Volunteer identification record:** The system shall support issuing and recording a form of volunteer identification approved by local officials (implemented digitally for the prototype). [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] **Won't**
* **FR-26 Screening (configurable flags only):** The system shall allow coordinators to (a) mark whether screening/background checks are required for a specific task and/or volunteer, and (b) record the screening status as Required and Completed (optionally with a free-text note), without implementing the screening process itself. [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] **Should**
* **FR-27 Coordinator search/filter (matching support):** The system shall support coordinator search/filtering of volunteers by skills and availability to support manual matching. [Volunteering matching application for a crisis management platform, Petrovic] [A Graph-Based Approach to Volunteer Management, Windsperger] **Could**
* **FR-28 Automated shift capping:** The system shall allow coordinators to set volunteer capacity limits per shift to automatically distribute the workload and reduce the cognitive effort of manual monitoring. ["We actually had to distribute the lot and uh, introduce the constraints, uh, that you cannot sign up if there are more than X people on the shift. And apparently good X was seven."] **Must**
* **FR-29 Recurring schedule automation:** The system shall allow coordinators to copy and paste shift structures to future days, removing the repetitive mental burden of manual weekly schedule creation. ["Doodle for planning could have could be much improved because we cannot really do something, like have the shifts that would be translating to the next days and just copy, copy, paste in there. So I would have to create this every week."] **Should**
* **FR-30 Dynamic task deactivation:** The system shall automatically remove or mark tasks as taken once filled, saving coordinators the emotional and cognitive labor of managing frustrated, double-booked volunteers. ["And then I made the, uh, half an hour trip for my own money, actually, because I was not even paid for the train fare to only realize that, Oh, there was a translator, after all... I would expect on the platform to see only the things that are not not yet taken care of."] **Must**
* **FR-31 Targeted task filtering engine:** The system shall provide volunteers with a filtering engine to sort available tasks by specific categories (e.g., location, required skills). This replaces chaotic chat-based notifications and allows volunteers to independently find suitable matches, reducing the manual routing workload for coordinators. ["But afterwards I realized that it was just too messy. It was like a lot of these channels, a lot of notifications, and it was not possible to keep up with all of them... you can filter through the requests maybe so like someone is requesting to have support with children... someone wants a translation"] **Must**

## Non-Functional Requirements (NFR)
* **NFR-01 Usability under pressure:** The system shall provide an interface that minimizes coordinator cognitive load by clearly visualizing the real-time status of all tasks (e.g., open, proposed, filled). The coordinator must be able to assess current volunteer capacity and task shortages at a glance, without needing to manually cross-reference external lists. [A Graph-Based Approach to Volunteer Management, Windsperger] [Volunteering matching application for a crisis management platform, Petrovic] **Must**
* **NFR-02 Surge scalability:** The system shall remain usable when large numbers of volunteers register in a short period. [Towards a Taxonomy for Classification of Coordination Systems, Betke et al.] [Volunteering matching application for a crisis management platform, Petrovic] **Should**
* **NFR-03 Fast access to current data:** The system shall make up-to-date task/volunteer data quickly available to support response decision-making. [Information Systems for Crisis Management, Ristvej & Zagorecki] **Should**
* **NFR-04 Auditability:** The system shall provide traceability via event history to reproduce actions after the incident. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Could**
* **NFR-05 Reporting & archiving:** The system shall be able to store data, archive it, and produce reports based on this data (e.g., assignments and hours). [Information Systems for Crisis Management, Ristvej & Zagorecki] [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] **Could**
* **NFR-06 Secure access (placeholder):** The system shall provide secure but easy and reliable access control appropriate for crisis operations (details to be defined). [Information Systems for Crisis Management, Ristvej & Zagorecki] **Should**
* **NFR-07 Data minimisation:** The system shall implement a fixed, minimal data schema (collecting only Name, Email, Skills, Availability) to adhere to data minimization principles without requiring complex configuration. [Towards a Taxonomy for Classification of Coordination Systems, Betke et al.] [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] [A Graph-Based Approach to Volunteer Management, Windsperger] **Must**
* **NFR-08 Accessibility & device independence:** The web UI shall be responsive for both coordinator and volunteer use. [A Graph-Based Approach to Volunteer Management, Windsperger] **Must**
* **NFR-09 Availability & continuity:** The system shall be designed so that data maintenance (keeping the database up to date) is operationally feasible for the municipality. [Information Systems for Crisis Management, Ristvej & Zagorecki] **Won't**
* **NFR-10 Explainability (recommended):** When presenting automatic/semi-automatic matches, the system shall present a brief explanation of why a volunteer was matched (e.g., matching skills/availability). [A Graph-Based Approach to Volunteer Management, Windsperger] [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Could**
* **NFR-11 Interoperability:** The system shall keep interfaces open for possible integration into existing mission command systems (out of prototype scope unless implemented). [Towards a Taxonomy for Classification of Coordination Systems, Betke et al.] [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Won't**
* **NFR-12 Need-to-know by design:** The system shall support limiting volunteer-facing information to reduce risks from self-coordinated behavior. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Should**
* **NFR-13 Modularity:** The prototype shall be structured into three major components: (1) Registration, (2) Matching/Allocation, and (3) Coordinator Dashboard. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Should**
* **NFR-14 Automated authorization protocols:** The system shall implement a user vetting process to shift the burden of security from the coordinator to the system, preventing malicious actors during an acute crisis phase. ["we had few cases with the suspicion that maybe the person was, uh, let's say, not a true volunteer, but would be hunting for people... We had to ban the people. So let's say some sort of authorization would be good."] **Could**
* **NFR-15 Single source of truth:** The system shall serve as a centralized, verified information repository to reduce the time coordinators spend untangling broken communication chains and correcting rumors. ["Another big story was that the information propagation is when when the chain of the propagation is longer than two, information gets completely destroyed."] **Must**

## Information Requirements (IR)
* **IR-01 Volunteer identity:** The system shall store volunteer identity and contact data (at minimum: name and email address) to enable registration and later communication. [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] **Must**
* **IR-02 Volunteer skills/capabilities:** The system shall store volunteers' self-reported skills/capabilities for skill-based matching. [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] [Volunteering matching application for a crisis management platform, Petrovic] **Must**
* **IR-03 Fixed capability schema:** The system shall capture a fixed set of capability checkboxes including physical work difficulty levels and information-related work (e.g., hard/medium/easy physical work; paper work; care; information retrieval). [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Should**
* **IR-04 Volunteer availability:** The system shall capture volunteer availability as time blocks or comparable structured availability entries. [An optimization model for humanitarian relief volunteer management, Falasca et al.] [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Must**
* **IR-05 Volunteer task preferences:** The system shall capture volunteer task preferences to support preference-aware matching. [An optimization model for humanitarian relief volunteer management, Falasca et al.] **Should**
* **IR-06 Volunteer location:** The system shall store volunteer location as text based regions (postal code or distinct identifiers) to support location-aware coordination and routing. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Could**
* **IR-07 Volunteer assignment status:** The system shall store volunteer assignment status per task (e.g., proposed/assigned/confirmed/on-site/completed/cancelled). [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Must**
* **IR-08 Task core fields:** The system shall store, per task, category, description, location, required skills, and volunteer capacity. [A Graph-Based Approach to Volunteer Management, Windsperger] **Must**
* **IR-09 Task schedule fields:** The system shall store task start/end time and/or scheduling windows for time-based matching. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Must**
* **IR-10 Task priority/urgency:** The system shall store a task priority/urgency level to support prioritised allocation in overload situations. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] [Decision Support for the Optimal Coordination of Spontaneous Volunteers, Rauchecker et al.] **Should**
* **IR-11 Task activities & per-activity demand:** The system shall allow tasks to include multiple activities and a per-activity demand for volunteers (splitting into subtasks). [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Won't**
* **IR-12 Documentation of work:** The system shall store volunteer work documentation including hours served and types of work performed. [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] **Could**
* **IR-13 Referral/assignment payload:** The system shall store referral details sent to volunteers (duties description, full address/location, and a contact point). [Management of Spontaneous Volunteers in Disasters, FEMA] **Should**
* **IR-14 Audit/event history:** The system shall store an event history (who did what, when) for later reconstruction and accountability. [A Volunteer Coordination System Approach for Crisis Committees, Betke] **Could**
